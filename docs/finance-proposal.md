## 2. Business rationale

### Problem to solve

AI-text detectors can falsely accuse human writers and can fail on short posts, unfamiliar generators, translations, and edited or mixed writing. Combining several detectors does not automatically improve reliability.

To select and calibrate a useful system, the project needs examples whose labels come from the **recorded writing process**, rather than another detector guessing whether the text looks AI-generated.

### Why existing data is insufficient

Existing public datasets can help with supplementary testing, but the sources investigated do not jointly provide the required combination of:

- Naturally short, contemporary social-style writing.
- Sufficient independent contributors and source groups.
- Recorded unaided, generated, rewritten, translated and human-edited workflows.
- Clear rights for the intended use.
- Suitable separation from detector training data and final evaluation.

Restrictions, older generators, uncertain human labels, longer-form writing and limited source-account counts remain issues. A large number of excerpts from a few authors does not constitute a large independent sample.

### Expected value of the pilot

The pilot could reduce the risk of committing substantial funds to an ineffective collection protocol by measuring data quality and collection cost. This benefit is **conditional**: if the product has no sufficient business value, the model is infeasible for the intended scope, or an existing solution is preferable, perfecting collection is not worth funding.

No revenue, return-on-investment figure, model accuracy improvement or production launch date is claimed in this proposal.

### Session workflow

1. **Information, consent and practice.** Explain the AI-detection purpose, task-scoped writing history, permissions and payment. Obtain affirmative, timestamped consent before saving task text or participant identifiers.
2. **Four unaided originals.** Collect new short replies, observations/jokes, arguments or nonconfidential technical explanations, and posts allowing natural emoji/link/quotation usage. Vary lengths and styles.
3. **Assistance control.** Record a mechanical-only or ideas-only workflow, preserving the original, actual assistance, changes or discarded wording, and final text.
4. **AI-edit task.** After unaided writing, present a recorded model draft through an authorized controlled route and retain the human editing process. This task must not be claimed as completed if no working authorized generation route is available.
5. **Debrief and completion.** Record actual tools used, resolve accidental personal/third-party content, and provide working payment and withdrawal routes.

Contributors should use invented or non-sensitive situations, not private conversations or confidential work.

Several posts and variants from one contributor remain related records. They are **not several independent contributors**.

### Pilot exclusions

This phase does not include:

- A statistically validated production authorship percentage.
- A definitive accuracy benchmark or proof of a rare false-positive rate.
- Deployment of a scoring profile into the extension.
- Public release of raw text or process records.
- Recruitment for the full study.
- A new GPU purchase.
- Paid external model APIs or transmission of contributor text to outside model providers without separate approval and appropriate participant permissions.

### Why the sample is large

Under simple independent-observation assumptions, zero observed false positives in about 600 negative observations gives a one-sided 95% upper bound near 0.5%. Related passages cannot be counted as independent observations.

This is an illustration, **not proof that the proposed study establishes that error rate on X**. Observed errors, dependencies, subgroup claims and population differences can require more data. The 50-group floors are engineering safeguards, not a complete statistical power analysis.

The full study would still not establish the actual prevalence of AI writing on X or guarantee representative performance across all languages, lengths, generators and editing conditions.

## 7. Data quality and review controls

- Assign contributor/source blocks before model evaluation; do not move them between partitions to improve results.
- Preserve shared authors, prompts, events, source drafts, edits and duplicates.
- Keep pilot contributors out of the untouched main-study evaluation population.
- Use two independent human reviewers, blinded to detector outputs.
- Map labels from writing-process evidence, not prose style or another AI detector.
- Treat substantive generated, rewritten, translated or retained AI wording as positive when supported.
- Treat unaided, mechanical-only and ideas-only writing as negative only when supported.
- Retain missing, ambiguous or disputed cases as **unknown**, not human.
- Record exclusions and group yield rather than inventing new identifiers to meet quotas.
- Keep payment decisions separate from benchmark usability. A detector score, paste event or focus change is not proof of misconduct.

The existing intake and adequacy tools enforce record consistency and count checks. They cannot authenticate a person, prove the truth of a declaration, establish legal rights, or guarantee independent reviewers.

### 18.3 Why “600” does not buy a guaranteed pass

The zero-error formula is about a **particular one-sided confidence bound for an independent sample**. The original software additionally uses a conservative event—any false flag within a negative connected group—which is not identical to an arbitrarily post-weighted population FPR.

With a true hypothetical FPR of 0.1%, the probability of observing zero errors in 600 independent negatives is only about **54.9%**. At a true 0.5%, it is about **4.94%**. Therefore, buying 600 observations does not guarantee the desired bound will pass, even for a relatively low-error model.

For comparison, zero-error illustrations require 299 independent negatives at 1% or 149 at 2%, with the same one-sided 95% assumptions. These are **not proposed unapproved target changes**, nor evidence those rates are acceptable.

Repeated posts cannot simply be treated as independent. Equally, the appropriate sampling unit and post-/author-/group-weighted estimand must be chosen explicitly; “600 observations means exactly 600 paid people in every partition” is not a universal theorem. A clustered/repeated-measures design requires defensible dependence assumptions and uncertainty calculations—not fabricated extra authors.

Do not keep buying data until a bound happens to pass. Any sequential testing/stopping or expansion design must be fixed appropriately in advance.

| Value and decision | Named user/use case, baseline alternative, plausible economic value, exact uncertainty the pilot resolves. | Do not fund a collection exercise without a worthwhile decision. |
| Feasibility and alternatives | Bounded local/hosted/source/partnership audit and an explanation of why this pilot is the best next experiment. | Reuse, redesign, narrow scope with approval, or stop. |
| Study design | Justified sampling unit, development/calibration/operating/final separation, cohort scope and labeling/review rules; explicit treatment of exploratory versus confirmatory adequacy. | Do not treat coded floors as a procurement specification. |
| Unit economics | Timed technical rehearsal where possible, review work breakdown, actual recruitment/review quotes and tax treatment; fully loaded resources identified. | No commitment based only on illustrative percentage arithmetic. |
| Readiness and rights | Approved controller/contact/terms, suitable collection platform, tested consent/payment/withdrawal/export/deletion and authorized generation route. | No participant recruitment. |
| Pilot acceptance and stops | Preregister record-integrity, review-quality, timing, pay, usable-group-yield and cost reporting; useful thresholds approved before commissioning. | No undefined “success” or automatic scale-up. |

Record integrity must include complete consent/permission/version references and exact source/output binding. Review reports must quantify missing/disputed cases by cohort. Full-scale planning must use uncertainty in group yield and cost, not only a favorable point estimate from 30 people. Human false positives, useful recall, coverage and probability scope must be agreed before a final detector test; the collection pilot is not that test.

**What has changed in this revision:** the investment recommendation, decision-history attribution, cost interpretation, alternatives and objection register. **What has not changed:** no funds committed, no platform account/recruitment/contact action, no new participant data, no operational code/gate changes, no production score or private GPU exposure. The original numerical scenario remains visible for audit, not as a predetermined outcome Finance is expected to endorse.
